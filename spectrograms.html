HARDWARE CAPABILITIES // MS-10

The Analogue Grinder

Routing sterile digital stems through the Korg MS-10's External Signal processor transforms the synthesiser into a highly characterful outboard effects unit. Below are the core treatment protocols available.

01.

Analogue Saturation & Overdrive

Hardware: Korg MS-10 Component: VCA / Ext In

Engineer Notes: By driving a digital track aggressively into the MS-10's External Signal In, we force the VCA and Korg-35 filter circuitry into musical clipping. This breaks up the signal, instantly adding rich harmonic distortion, weight, and grit to thin basslines or sterile drum tracks. Even with the filter fully open, the analogue signal path inherently rounds off harsh digital highs and warms the low-mids.

02.

Korg-35 Filter Dynamics

Hardware: Korg MS-10 Component: Korg-35 LPF / Mod Generator

Engineer Notes: The aggressive Korg-35 Low-Pass Filter is utilised in two primary ways. Manual Groove Riding: Real-time manipulation of the Cutoff and Resonance knobs to inject human feel, evolving movement, and aggressive squelch into static loops. Rhythmic LFO Sweeps: Patching the Modulation Generator (LFO) to the filter cutoff to apply cyclic, synchronised movement—ideal for creating pulsating pads or classic auto-wah effects on guitars and soft-synths.

WET // LFO Sweeping LPF 0:00 / 0:04
03.

VCA Gating & Rhythmic Chopping

Hardware: Korg MS-10 Component: VCA Initial Gain / Patchbay

Engineer Notes: By patching a square wave from the Modulation Generator (or an external trigger pulse) directly into the VCA Initial Gain input, we rhythmically mute and unmute the external audio. This transforms sustained drones, ambient noise, or long synth chords into tight, percussive rhythmic sequences.

04.

Transient Reshaping & Lo-Fi Noise

Hardware: Korg MS-10 Component: Envelope Generator / Noise Gen

Engineer Notes: Transient Reshaping: The Envelope Generator (EG) modulates the filter cutoff, snapping it open and closed rapidly via an external trigger to add sharp, synthetic attack transients to dull acoustic drums. Noise Texturing: The onboard White or Pink Noise generator is blended with the external input. Filtering them together glues the track to a bed of analogue hiss, effectively simulating degraded tape or vintage sampler grit.
